## 1.1 Scope [P-SCOPE]
These regulations govern the weekly teaching timetable of the department:
lectures, tutorials and practicals for all sections. Examinations, events
and room bookings for non-teaching purposes are outside their scope.

## 1.2 Who may request changes [P-AUTHORITY]
A faculty member may request changes only to their own classes. The Head of
Department may request changes for any class of the department. The lab
in-charge reports the availability of laboratories. The timetable
coordinator publishes the timetable. Students may report clashes through
their class representative but may not request that a class be moved or
cancelled.

## 2.1 Working hours [P-HOURS]
Teaching takes place Monday to Friday between 9:00 and 17:00, in eight
one-hour slots. No classes are held on Saturdays or Sundays. A class outside
these hours, or on a weekend, requires the prior written approval of the Head
of Department.

## 2.2 Common lunch break [P-LUNCH]
The hour from 13:00 to 14:00 is a common lunch break for all students and
staff. No lecture, tutorial or practical may be scheduled in this hour. The
lunch break cannot be waived by the department; only the Dean may grant an
exception.

## 2.3 Consecutive teaching hours [P-MAXCONSEC]
No faculty member and no section may be scheduled for more than three
consecutive teaching hours. After three hours in a row (back-to-back classes)
there must be a break of at least one hour. A practical session counts one
hour for each slot it occupies.

## 2.4 Contact hours and credits [P-CREDITS]
Contact hours follow the L-T-P structure of each course. A 4-credit theory
course has four contact hours per week (three lectures and one tutorial); a
4-credit course with a laboratory has three lectures and one two-hour
practical. All contact hours must appear in the weekly timetable.

## 2.5 Teaching load [P-LOAD]
A full-time faculty member teaches at most sixteen contact hours per week.
Guest faculty teach at most eight contact hours per week.

## 3.1 Teaching preferences [P-PREFS]
Faculty may state preferences about days and times, such as keeping mornings
or a particular day free. Preferences are accommodated where the timetable
allows, but they are not guaranteed and give way to commitments and to other
faculty members' unavailability.

## 3.2 Make-up classes [P-MAKEUP]
A class missed because of the faculty member's absence (conference, leave,
official duty or a personal appointment) must be rescheduled as a make-up
class within two weeks of the missed class. Recurring unavailability declared
for the whole semester creates no make-up obligation, because the timetable is
built around it.

## 3.3 Swapping slots [P-SWAP]
Two faculty members may swap teaching slots with the consent of both. The
timetable coordinator must be informed before the swap takes effect, and the
swap must not break any other regulation.

## 3.4 Online and covered classes [P-ONLINE]
A class may be held online, or taken by a colleague, only as a make-up
arrangement and only with the approval of the Head of Department. No course
may have more than two online classes in a semester.

## 4.1 Laboratories and equipment [P-LABS]
Practical sessions are held in laboratories with the equipment the course
requires. The lab in-charge reports laboratory closures for maintenance, and
the sessions affected are moved to another suitable laboratory or slot.

## 4.2 Room capacity [P-CAPACITY]
A class must be held in a room whose capacity is at least the number of
students registered in the section.

## 4.3 Event and room bookings [P-BOOKING]
Bookings of the auditorium, seminar halls or rooms for events, fests and
meetings are handled by the Estate Office, not by the timetable office.

## 5.1 Clash reports [P-CLASH]
Students who find that two of their registered courses clash report it
through their class representative. The timetable office investigates every
report within five working days and informs the students of the outcome.

## 6.1 Confidentiality [P-PRIVACY]
Reasons given for unavailability, such as medical or family matters, are
confidential. The timetable office records only that a reason was given and,
where required, verified; the reason itself is never published or shared.
